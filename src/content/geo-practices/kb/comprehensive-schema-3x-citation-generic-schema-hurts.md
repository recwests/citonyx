## Summary
Pages with comprehensive, well-implemented schema markup are roughly 3x more likely to earn AI citations. But generic boilerplate schema actually lowers citation odds to 41.6% vs 59.8% for sites with no schema — precision matters more than presence. Evidence on stacking specific types is conflicting.

## Details
Audit of 500+ sites found comprehensive schema pages are about 3x more likely to earn AI citations. Separately, a Growth Marshal study of 730 sites found generic boilerplate schema drops citation probability to 41.6% vs 59.8% for no schema. Key schema priority order: (1) Organization — foundational entity anchor; (2) FAQPage — highest citation impact, pre-structured answers AI can extract verbatim; (3) Article/BlogPosting — signals authorship and freshness; (4) Person — verifies author credibility; (5) Dataset — makes original research machine-readable. Critical underused properties: sameAs (links to LinkedIn, Crunchbase, G2 — fewer than 3 links is a missed opportunity) and knowsAbout (declares topical expertise). The connected graph approach — using @id to link Article, Person and Organization — changes how crawlers interpret the site.
Conflicting 2026 claim (Digital Applied): pages stacking Article, BreadcrumbList and Organization markup report 3.1x more AI citations than single or absent schema — but pages with complete schema also tend to be newer, better structured and more authoritative, and controlled schema studies (Ahrefs, Otterly AI) find near-zero independent lift. Safe reading: schema helps machine parsing and entity clarity but is not a standalone citation lever; do not promise a threefold lift from markup alone.
