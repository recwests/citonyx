## Summary
Brandi AI analyzed ~900K AI answers across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, and Copilot. The 25 most-cited human-written pages earned 23% of all citations (682 avg/page), while the top 25 AI-written pages captured just 4% (119 avg/page) — a 5.7x gap.

## Details
Conducted in Q2 2026, the study tracked 4,210 webpages across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Google Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot. The top 1% of human-written pages captured 27% of all citations; the top 1% of AI-written pages captured less than 1%. Among pages still receiving citations in the final month, human-written pages averaged 37% more citations per day (1.80 vs 1.31). The most-cited human-written pages shared characteristics difficult to reproduce with AI: original research, proprietary data, firsthand experience, subject-matter expertise, and distinctive conclusions. The finding challenges the assumption that increasing content output alone increases AI visibility. Brandi AI recommends organizations use AI for research and structure while concentrating human expertise on distinctive, evidence-based information.
