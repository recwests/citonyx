## Summary
The first randomized field experiment on AI Overviews (Indian School of Business and Carnegie Mellon, SSRN, 1,065 US users, 68,089 searches) found hiding the overview raised outbound clicks from 0.37 to 0.62 per search — a 39.8% reduction attributable to AI Overviews (reported as a 38% cut in organic clicks), while zero-click outcomes rose 33%.

## Details
- Causal design via Chrome extension with three conditions: normal Search, Search without AI Overviews, and AI Mode; authors call it the first randomized experiment isolating AI Overviews' causal click effect.
- Removing AI Overviews left user satisfaction, perceived quality, and ease of finding information unchanged — undercutting Google's "bounce clicks" narrative: the lost clicks were not merely low-value visits.
- The effect concentrates on informational queries (~0.26 extra clicks per search); navigational and transactional estimates were inconclusive.
- Prior studies (Seer, Chartbeat, Pew) were observational or correlational; the randomized design supports causal claims.
- Caveats: working paper, single study, small self-selected panel, not peer-reviewed. Correlational data suggests some CTR recovery by early 2026 (Seer: ~0.61% to ~2.4% on AI Overview queries).
- Related 2026 signals: Ahrefs — top result CTR drops ~58% when an AI Overview is present; BrightEdge measured AIO on ~48% of tracked queries (Healthcare 88%); Pew — users click a result on 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without; SparkToro — 68% of US searches end without a click.
