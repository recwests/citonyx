## Summary
Google's official AI optimization guide (updated Jul 2026) states generative AI features are rooted in the core Search index via RAG and query fan-out, so classic SEO still applies. Key positions: no special file, hidden markup, or separate protocol for generative AI. GEO/AEO framed as 'terms you may see used' — from Google's perspective it's still SEO. llms.txt? Google doesn't use it. Content chunking? Not required. Special AI schema? No separate protocol needed.

## Details
Ahrefs data confirms only 3% of llms.txt implementations get any traffic; AI retrieval bots = 1.1% of requests (Slackbot fetched more than PerplexityBot). Google recommends: standard SEO best practices, unique non-commodity expert-led content with a first-hand point of view, clear crawlable technical structure. Warns against: inauthentic mentions, fake best-of lists, recommendation poisoning, scaled doorway pages, schema contradicting visible content. For local and commerce surfaces, maintain Google Business Profile and Merchant Center. May 2026 spam policies now explicitly cover attempts to manipulate generative AI responses; the new Generative AI performance report in Search Console measures results. Scope: Google-specific — llms.txt is still consumed by Perplexity and Claude. The practical implication: invest in SEO fundamentals before GEO-specific tactics.
