## Summary
Review velocity (rate of new reviews per month) correlates r=0.72 with AI citation rates vs r=0.31 for average star rating. A later G2 study points the same way: top-quartile review count predicted 7.6x more AI Overview mentions while top-quartile star rating produced 0.9x. AI treats review volume and flow as ongoing proof, not as a score to optimise.

## Details
50+ reviews is the minimum threshold before AI engines consider the review set statistically meaningful. After 50 reviews, velocity (2+ new reviews per week) matters more than absolute score. A brand with 100 reviews at 4.5 stars but no recent activity may be outranked by a competitor with 60 reviews at 4.2 stars earning 3 new reviews per week.

- Second evidence point: Minuttia and Convex Works compared 24,067 prompts with G2 data. Top-quartile review count brands appeared 7.6x more often on category prompts and 5.9x on alternative prompts than bottom-quartile brands; top-quartile star rating gave 0.9x, i.e. no association.
- Caveats: G2 covered 63% of the studied brands (sample skews to established SaaS), figures are associations from a single snapshot, and volume most likely proxies reputation rather than causing citation.
- Practical read: resource review-count growth on third-party platforms as an entity signal and stop treating star rating as an AI-visibility lever.
