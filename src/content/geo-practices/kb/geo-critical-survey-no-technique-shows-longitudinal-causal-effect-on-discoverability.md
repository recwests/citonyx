## Summary
A critical survey of 45 GEO studies (2023–2026) argues GEO is not a single ranking task but a stochastic pipeline. Foundational gains are valid experimentally but conditional on already-retrieved content — no reviewed technique shows stable longitudinal cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability.

## Details
Olivier Martinez's survey (arXiv:2607.14035, July 2026) reviews 45 GEO studies published Nov 2023–July 2026 and argues GEO is not a single ranking task but a stochastic pipeline. Foundational gains are valid experimentally but conditional on already-retrieved content — no reviewed technique shows stable longitudinal cross-platform causal effect on organic discoverability. The survey introduces a multistage formal model (a visibility vector) separating discoverability, citation, absorption, and economic outcomes. It rejects the blanket claim "GEO increases visibility by 40%" as a relative maximum under one configuration. Topical relevance and context position are the most reproducible levers. Generic heuristics transfer poorly, competition can erode gains, and citation-oriented rewrites can impair retrieval. Commercial audits reveal low source overlap and persistent fidelity gaps. Introduces an evidence hierarchy (randomized field trials highest; commercial-engine repetition; black-box post-retrieval) and recommends measuring across multiple engines and query classes rather than trusting single prompt runs or tool scores — with a reproducible protocol of repeated measurements, paraphrases, controls and human validation.
