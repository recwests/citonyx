## Summary
On May 15, 2026, Google published its first official guide on optimizing for generative AI features in Search. Key pillars: (1) quality content that demonstrates first-hand expertise, (2) E-E-A-T signals (author credentials, expert citations), (3) entity consistency across the web so Google can connect brand references. Structured data is explicitly NOT required for AI Overviews or AI Mode; llms.txt and content chunking are debunked. SEO remains the foundation; GEO adds AI-specific layers.

## Details
- Google's framework: traditional SEO + structured data (helpful, not required) + E-E-A-T + entity consistency; entity consistency means brand name, logo, social profiles, and descriptions identical across platforms.
- FAQ rich results were deprecated on May 7, 2026; a May 2026 SEJ-reported study found JSON-LD did not measurably increase AI citations.
- Keep Q&A content format regardless of markup: FAQ sections have the highest citation probability of any format (~81% per GetCite's 10,000-page analysis), independent of FAQPage schema.
- Trakkr's 28,000+ citation study: 68% of AI-cited pages have structured data (double the web average), yet schema types don't predict citation volume — content quality and structure matter more.
- This conflicts with earlier 2026 guidance (e.g., Contently's February guide) treating schema as a discovery baseline — current evidence points to substance over markup.
- Writer's practical rule: keep JSON-LD for rich results and entity clarity, but do not treat it as an AI-citation lever.
- llms.txt: NOT mentioned by Google — confirming Google does not consider it relevant.
